Welcome to Module 4: Cataloging, Resource Records & Collection Management of the Follett Destiny Library Management System (LMS) training program. Building upon system navigation, security structures, and patron administration established in Modules 1–3, this module equips new school librarians with comprehensive technical skills to build, organize, and maintain a high-quality digital and physical library catalog (Follett School Solutions, "Add Title").

Accurate cataloging and standardized Machine-Readable Cataloging (MARC) data management form the structural foundation of library discoverability, inventory precision, and student search success. In the latest Destiny 24.0 release, Follett introduces the Library AI Assistant, a conversational assistant librarians can query for collection insights and reporting (covered in Module 7) alongside cloud-based Alliance Plus z39.50 cataloging workflows (Follett Software, "What's Coming").

What You'll Learn

  • Search Alliance Plus and Z39.50 sources before creating a manual catalog record, in order to prevent duplicates.
  • Select the appropriate cataloging tool (Easy Editor or MARC Editor) for a given title.
  • Apply the CREW/MUSTIE framework to evaluate a physical item as a weeding candidate.
  • Execute an individual and a batch weed in Destiny, including the correct Weeding Reason and Disposal Method.
  • Correctly catalog a multi-volume series using MARC tags 490/830 or 245 $n/$p as appropriate.

2. Cataloging Workflows & Sourcing MARC Records

Librarians manage collection cataloging by navigating to Back Office > Catalog > Add Title (Follett School Solutions, "Add Title"). Primary record sourcing and creation workflows include:

A. Alliance Plus & Cloud Z39.50 Sourcing

  • Alliance Plus Search: Follett’s cloud-hosted bibliographic database containing millions of pre-cataloged MARC records. Librarians query Alliance Plus by 13-digit ISBN, ISSN, title, or author (Follett School Solutions, "Add Title").
  • Standardized Metadata Sourcing: Automatically imports verified Library of Congress Subject Headings (LCSH), Sears Subject Headings, Dewey Decimal Classification (DDC) numbers, and publisher metadata to eliminate manual data entry errors.

B. Using the Library AI Assistant for Collection-Development Decisions

  • Collection-Gap Insights Before Ordering: Before finalizing a new title order, librarians can ask the Library AI Assistant about gaps or high-demand areas in a given subject or genre, rather than relying on manual report-building (Follett Software, "Library AI Assistant").
  • Weeding & Purchasing Worklists: The Library AI Assistant can generate automated weeding and purchasing worklists, giving catalogers a starting point that still requires professional review before action (Follett Software, "Library AI Assistant").

C. Manual Title Creation & Local MARC 21 Editing

When a title is not available in Alliance Plus, librarians create original records via Catalog > Add Title > New Title and edit local MARC 21 tags (Follett School Solutions, "Add Title"):

  • Tag 020: International Standard Book Number (ISBN).
  • Tag 100: Primary Author / Main Entry Personal Name.
  • Tag 245: Title and Statement of Responsibility (Title, Subtitle, Subfield $a and $b).
  • Tag 260 / 264: Publication, Distribution, and Copyright Information (Place, Publisher, Date).
  • Tag 300: Physical Description (Page count, illustrations, dimensions).
  • Tag 490 / 830: Series Statement and Series Added Entry.
  • Tag 520: Summary / Annotation note.
  • Tag 650: Topical Subject Headings (Crucial for keyword search indexing).

3. Copy Management, Physical Call Numbers & Sublocations

A title record represents the intellectual work, while Copy Records represent individual physical or digital items owned by the school library (Follett School Solutions, "Add Title"):

A. Physical Copy Administration (Add Copy)

  • Barcode Assignment: Scanning a unique 14-digit barcode label affixed to the physical book cover or flyleaf.
  • Circulation Types: Assigning loan rules (e.g., Regular Library, Reference, Overnight, Teacher Resource) which dictate checkout durations and renewal limits.
  • Financial & Vendor Metrics: Recording purchase price, replacement cost, acquisition date, vendor name, and funding source for budget auditing and loss reporting.

B. Call Numbers & Dewey Decimal Classification (DDC)

  • Establishing standardized call number prefixes and classification strings (e.g., FIC ROW for fiction, 530 HAW for non-fiction physics, REF 912 for reference atlases) to ensure logical shelf placement and intuitive patron wayfinding.

C. Sublocation Mapping & Genrefication

  • Sublocation Zoning: Mapping copy records to localized library zones (e.g., Graphic Novels, Picture Books, Biographies, STEM Corner, Teacher Professional) in Destiny Back Office.
  • Genrefication Support: Assigning sublocations enables students searching in Destiny Discover to filter titles by physical genre sections across the facility.

D. Digital Assets & WebPath Express Integration

  • Digital eBooks & Audiobooks: Integrating Follett eBooks and interactive digital subscriptions directly into the catalog grid for instant single-sign-on (SSO) streaming in Destiny Discover.
  • WebPath Express: Activating curated, educator-reviewed internet research links matched to curriculum topics and reading levels (Follett School Solutions).

Module 4 Supplement: Weeding Workflows, Label Printing, Bulk MARC Imports & Series Cataloging

A. Weeding & Deselection Workflow (The CREW Method)

Collection maintenance requires systematic deselection to remove outdated, damaged, or uncirculated materials:

  • The CREW Method & MUSTIE Criteria: Based on the Texas State Library and Archives Commission (TSLAC) CREW guidelines, librarians evaluate candidates using the MUSTIE framework:
    • Misleading (factually inaccurate or outdated science/geography).
    • Ugly (worn, damaged, stained, or torn pages).
    • Superseded (newer edition available).
    • Trivial (lacks literary or educational merit).
    • Irrelevant to curriculum or student needs.
    • Elsewhere (readily available via Interlibrary Loan or digital databases) (Follett Software, "Simplify Your Library Management").
  • Destiny Weeding Execution (Catalog > Update Copies):
    • Individual Weeding (Update Copies > Individual Delete): Scan single copy barcodes, enable the "Track as weeded" checkbox, and select a Weeding Reason (e.g., Damaged, Outdated, Lost, Duplicate) and Disposal Method (e.g. Discarded, Donated, Recycled) (Follett School Solutions, "Update Copies").
    • Batch Weeding (Update Copies > Batch Delete): Upload a text file of barcodes (collected via Follett Remote or VersaScan) to weed an entire shelf or cart simultaneously (Follett School Solutions, "Update Copies").
    • Last Copy Configuration (Admin > Site Configuration): Selecting "When the last copy of a title is deleted, remove the title from the library's collection" automatically purges orphan MARC records (Follett School Solutions, "Copies").
  • Weeding Log Audit Report (Reports > Library Reports > Weeding Log): Generates audit reports detailing weeded titles, copy barcodes, call numbers, weeding reasons, and total collection value discarded (Follett Software, "Generate a Weeding Log").

B. Spine & Barcode Label Printing Workflow

Librarians generate physical labels for newly cataloged items or replacement spine tags (Reports > Library Reports > Labels) (Follett School Solutions, "Barcode Labels"):

  • Barcode Labels (Reports > Library Reports > Barcode Labels): Outputs barcode labels for specific copies, barcode ranges, or unused barcode label stock (e.g., Avery 5160 30-up or Demco 8-up labels). Includes site name, title, call number, and barcode image (Follett School Solutions, "Barcode Labels").
  • Spine & Pocket Labels (Reports > Library Reports > Spine/Pocket Labels): Prints call numbers, sublocation prefixes, and author codes for book spines. Sorted by Call Number or Date/Time Added (Follett School Solutions, "Spine and Pocket Labels").
  • Printer Offset & Alignment: Configures top and left margin offsets in label setup options to ensure accurate label alignment across various laser/inkjet printers (iLTSS).

C. Bulk MARC Import & Retrospective Collection Integration

When acquiring retrospective book collections or vendor shipments (e.g., Titlewave, OverDrive, Baker & Taylor), librarians import batch MARC files (Catalog > Import Titles > Add/Update) (Follett Community, "Importing Title Records"):

  • Title Matching Rules:
    • Strict Matching: Requires exact matches on standard numbers (LCCN, ISBN-10/13, ISSN) plus title and material type. Prevents duplicate catalog records (Follett School Solutions, "Import title records").
    • Relaxed Matching: Matches on Title, Author, Publication Date, and Material Type when standard numbers are absent (Follett School Solutions, "Import title records").
  • MARC 852 Tag Processing: Destiny parses embedded MARC 852 holdings tags to automatically generate copy barcode numbers, call numbers, sublocations, purchase prices, and vendor data upon import (Follett School Solutions, "Importing 852 holdings tag").

D. Multi-Volume & Series Cataloging Best Practices

Proper series cataloging ensures sequential books and multi-volume sets display correctly in student search results:

  • Series Metadata Entry:
    • Easy Editor: Series title and volume numbering are entered on the Series/Notes sub-tab (Follett School Solutions, "Add Title Record").
    • MARC 21 Series Tags:
      • Tag 490: Series statement as transcribed directly from the item.
      • Tag 830: Authorized uniform series title and volume number (e.g., $a Percy Jackson & the Olympians ;$v bk. 1) (Follett Community, "Ask Ms. MARC").
      • Tag 245 $n / $p: Multi-volume set titles (e.g. $a World Book Encyclopedia. $n Volume 5, $p C-Ch) (Follett Community, "Cataloging Multivolume Sets").
  • Multivolume Cataloging Approaches:
    • Single Bibliographic Record: Catalog the entire set under one MARC record, list volume titles in MARC 505 contents tag, and attach separate copy barcodes for each physical volume (Follett Community, "Cataloging Multivolume Sets").
    • Separate Bibliographic Records: Create individual title records for each volume when individual titles or authors vary significantly across volumes (Follett Community, "Cataloging Multivolume Sets").
  • Destiny 24.0 Series Visibility: Series names and volume numbers display prominently in Destiny Discover search results, copy details, and hold queues so students can verify reading order instantly (Follett Software, "Destiny 24.0: Product Updates").

5. Practical Hands-On Learning Activities

To complete Module 4, new librarians should perform the following hands-on exercises in their Destiny sandbox or live site:

  • Activity 4.1 - Alliance Plus Search & Title Sourcing: Navigate to Back Office > Catalog > Add Title, set the search dropdown to ISBN, scan or enter a 13-digit ISBN for a test book, select the matching Alliance Plus record, review its MARC tags, and click Save Title (Tech Librarian Helpdesk; Follett School Solutions, "Add Title").
  • Activity 4.2 - Copy Registration & Sublocation Assignment: Inside the saved Title Details view, click Add Copy. Scan a fresh barcode, set the Call Number to FIC ROW, select the Sublocation as Graphic Novels, set the Circulation Type to Regular Library, enter the acquisition price, and click Save Copy (Follett School Solutions, "Add Title").
  • Activity 4.3 - Local MARC Editing: Search for an existing title record in Back Office > Catalog, click Edit Title, manually add a missing summary and interest level, add a custom topical subject heading (MARC Tag 650), and click Save Title (Follett Software, "Destiny 24.0: Product Updates").

Q1. Before manually creating a new title record, what should a librarian always do first?

  • A. Assign a call number
  • B. Search Alliance Plus or a Z39.50 source to check for an existing record
  • C. Print spine labels
  • D. Run the Weeding Log report

Correct answer: B

Q2. A book is factually outdated in a way that could mislead students, but otherwise in good physical condition. Which MUSTIE criterion applies?

  • A. Ugly
  • B. Superseded
  • C. Misleading
  • D. Trivial
Show answer
Correct answer: C

Q3. Which MARC tag holds the authorized uniform series title and volume number (e.g., "Percy Jackson ; bk. 1")?

  • A. Tag 245
  • B. Tag 490
  • C. Tag 830
  • D. Tag 650
Show answer
Correct answer: C

Q4. What does enabling "When the last copy of a title is deleted, remove the title from the library's collection" do?

  • A. Automatically purges orphaned MARC records with no remaining copies
  • B. Deletes all copies of every title in that sublocation
  • C. Disables the Weeding Log report
  • D. Converts the title to a digital-only record
Show answer
Correct answer: A

Q5. A shipment of retrospective titles is being bulk-imported via MARC files. Explain the difference between Strict and Relaxed title-matching rules and when you'd use each.

Show key points
Strict matching requires exact matches on standard numbers (ISBN/ISSN/LCCN) plus title and material type, preventing duplicates when standard numbers are reliable; relaxed matching falls back to Title/Author/Publication Date/Material Type when standard numbers are missing, useful for older or less-standardized records.

Q6. You are cataloging a 6-volume encyclopedia set where each volume covers different content but shares one title. Which multivolume cataloging approach would you choose, and why?

Show key points
Single Bibliographic Record with MARC 505 contents tag listing volume titles and separate copy barcodes per volume — appropriate when the volumes share one overarching title/author, rather than creating separate bib records (which is reserved for volumes with significantly different titles/authors).
