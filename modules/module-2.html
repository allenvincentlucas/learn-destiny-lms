Welcome to Module 2: System Structure, Hierarchy & Access Levels of the Follett Destiny Library Management System (LMS) training program. Building upon the basic navigation concepts established in Module 1, this module equips new school librarians with a comprehensive understanding of Destiny's security architecture, district-to-site administrative structure, and user permission configurations (Follett School Solutions, &quot;Key Roles&quot;).

Proper administrative setup ensures data privacy, prevents unauthorized catalog modifications, and maintains operational integrity across library and asset management workflows (Follett School Solutions, &quot;Access Levels&quot;).

What You'll Learn

  • Differentiate district-level and site-level administrative roles within Destiny's security hierarchy.
  • Configure a custom access level with appropriate permissions across the four sub-tabs (Library Materials, Resources, Patrons, General).
  • Explain why FERPA/COPPA compliance requires restricting access to patron PII by role.
  • Set up a self-checkout station with privacy protections enabled.
  • Predict the outcome of a district "Push" operation on a site's local access-level settings.

2. Destiny System Hierarchy & User Roles

Destiny utilizes a multi-tiered security model that separates global district administration from site-level library management (Follett School Solutions, &quot;Define District Access Levels&quot;):

A. District-Level Administration

  • Destiny Administrator: Holds full administrative control across all schools within the school district. Responsibilities include setting district-wide options, establishing standardized access levels, overseeing district cataloging, and managing warehouse inventories (Follett School Solutions, &quot;Key Roles&quot;).
  • District Specialized Roles: Default installation roles tailored for centralized staff, including District Library Cataloger, District Textbooks Manager, District Patrons Manager, and District Reports User (Follett School Solutions, &quot;Access Levels&quot;).

B. Site-Level Administration

  • Site Administrator / Library Administrator: Controls administrative functions for a specific school building. Manages site loan policies, local calendar closures, patron access level assignments, and site-specific circulation rules (Follett School Solutions, &quot;Define District Access Levels&quot;).
  • Default Site Access Levels: Standard pre-configured roles installed at each school site (Follett School Solutions, &quot;Access Levels&quot;):
    • 1. Administrator: Full site-level access.
    • 2. Library Administrator: Full library management rights.
    • 3. Resource Administrator: Asset tracking and equipment rights.
    • 4. Teacher: Classroom booking and student resource management rights.
    • 5. Staff: General employee viewing and circulation rights.
    • 6. Patron: Basic student catalog search, hold placement, and personal checkout history.
    • 7. Guest: Read-only catalog search without personal profile access.

3. Configuring Access Levels and Sub-Tab Permissions

Access levels regulate what users can see and do within Destiny. Administrators manage permissions by navigating to Admin > Access Levels (Follett School Solutions, &quot;Access Levels&quot;). Each access level contains customizable permission checkboxes organized across four primary sub-tabs (Follett School Solutions, &quot;Access Levels&quot;):

  • 1. Library Materials Sub-Tab:
    • Controls search visibility, catalog editing rights, copy adding/deleting, hold overrides, and interlibrary loan permissions.
    • Regulates who can modify MARC records, import title lists, or access One Search configuration (Follett School Solutions, &quot;Access Levels&quot;).
  • 2. Resources Sub-Tab:
    • Configures rights for Destiny Resource Manager and IT Asset Manager.
    • Controls template creation, eSignature transfer approvals, and asset group assignments for 1-to-1 student laptops and equipment (Follett Software, &quot;What's Coming&quot;).
  • 3. Patrons Sub-Tab:
    • Determines permissions for viewing, adding, editing, merging, or deleting patron account records.
    • Controls access to sensitive student contact information, fine histories, and barcode assignments (Follett School Solutions, &quot;Access Levels&quot;).
  • 4. General Sub-Tab:
    • Manages system-wide administrative options, report creation rights, and access to Report Builder 2.0 (Follett Community, &quot;Destiny 23.5 Readiness Kit&quot;).
    • Security &amp; Session Timeout Controls: Specifies automatic idle timeout durations for user accounts (e.g., 5–15 minutes) and restricts self-checkout station displays to protect student privacy (Follett School Solutions, &quot;Access Levels&quot;).

Module 2 Supplement: Privacy Compliance, Self-Checkout Setup, LDAP Role Provisioning & Conflict Resolution

A. FERPA & Student Data Privacy Rationale

Access level management is fundamentally a student privacy and legal compliance necessity:

  • FERPA & COPPA Alignment: Under FERPA, a student's library circulation history, fines, and contact information are part of their education record — owned by the school district, not by Destiny as a vendor, and legally protected from disclosure without consent or a legitimate educational need (Follett Software, "Privacy Policy").
  • Role-Based Privacy Safeguards: Restricting permissions prevents unneeded exposure of Personally Identifiable Information (PII). For example, substitute teachers or volunteers granted "View library items out" rights should not be given access to full student demographic profiles or administrative job logs (Follett Community, "Access levels and student privacy").
  • Patron History Protection: Access levels restrict access to checkout history logs to protect student reading privacy and prevent unauthorized browsing disclosures (Follett Software, "Manage Technology").

B. Self-Checkout Station & Guest Mode Privacy Configuration

To maintain privacy at public or unmonitored library kiosks:

  • Self-Checkout Kiosk Setup (Circulation > Self-Checkout / Admin > Site Configuration): Dedicated terminal setup allowing students to check out items independently (Follett School Solutions, "Set Up a Self-Checkout Station").
  • Privacy Protections: Administrators enable the Display limited circulation information (Library only) checkbox under Admin > Access Levels, restricting on-screen displays strictly to the student's first name, cover thumbnail, and current checked-out item—masking full surnames, home addresses, and fine histories (Follett School Solutions, "Set Up a Self-Checkout Station").
  • Inactivity Auto-Reset: Automatically resets terminal displays after 15–30 seconds of inactivity to protect student transaction privacy from subsequent users (Follett School Solutions, "Access Levels").
  • Guest Mode Privileges: Unauthenticated guest access is restricted to read-only catalog searching and viewing public Collections. Guests cannot place holds, view patron profiles, check out items, or access Back Office administrative tabs (Follett Software, "Set Access Levels").

C. LDAP & SSO-Linked Automated Role Provisioning

Districts utilizing Active Directory (AD), OpenLDAP, or SSO authentication can automate access level management (Setup > District Options > LDAP / SSO Configuration) (My Catholic School):

  • Directory Attribute Mapping: User directory attributes (e.g. OU=Teachers, memberOf=Faculty, title=Librarian) map directly to Destiny Access Levels and Patron Types during bulk imports or real-time logins (Follett School Solutions, "Configure LDAP Servers").
  • Automated Lifecycle Management: When a staff member's directory status changes in Active Directory, Destiny automatically updates their access permissions upon their next login, eliminating manual role adjustments (My Catholic School).

D. District vs. Site-Level Conflict Resolution & Push Rules

Destiny offers two administrative control models to resolve policy conflicts between district administrators and school sites (Setup > District Options > Access Levels) (Follett School Solutions, "Define District Access Levels"):

  • 1. Setup Initial Access Levels (Site Autonomy): The district establishes initial baseline access levels for new schools. Site Administrators retain full authority to modify permissions locally for their specific building (Follett School Solutions, "Define District Access Levels").
  • 2. District Defined Access Levels Applied to All Sites (Central Control): The district locks access levels globally across all schools. Site Administrators can assign roles to patrons but cannot alter underlying permission checkmarks (Follett School Solutions, "Define District Access Levels").
  • 3. Push Conflict Overrides: District Administrators can execute a Push operation from Setup > District Options > Access Levels, forcibly overwriting site-level permission settings for specific schools or the entire district (Follett School Solutions, "Define District Access Levels").
    • Override Rules: Pushing access levels overwrites local permission checkmarks, but preserves site-created custom access levels, local resource groups, and site-specific inactivity timeouts (Follett School Solutions, "Define District Access Levels").

5. Practical Hands-On Learning Activities

To complete Module 2, new librarians should perform the following administrative exercises in their Destiny sandbox or live site:

  • Activity 2.1 - Access Level Permission Audit: Log into Destiny Back Office as a Site Administrator, navigate to Admin > Access Levels, and inspect the enabled permissions for Teacher, Staff, and Patron across all four sub-tabs (Follett School Solutions, &quot;Access Levels&quot;).
  • Activity 2.2 - Creating a Custom Student Assistant Role: Click Add Access Level under Admin > Access Levels. Name the new role &quot;Student Library Assistant&quot;, enable basic circulation and shelving permissions under the Library Materials sub-tab, set an idle timeout of 5 minutes, and save the role (Follett School Solutions, &quot;Access Levels&quot;).
  • Activity 2.3 - Patron Access Level Assignment: Navigate to Back Office > Patrons, search for a test student account, edit their record to assign the newly created &quot;Student Library Assistant&quot; access level, and verify their updated interface privileges (readlearngrow14).

Q5. A substitute teacher only needs to see which items are checked out to their homeroom, nothing else. What access-level principle should guide how you configure their permissions, and why?

Show key points
Apply the principle of least privilege / role-based privacy safeguards: grant only "View library items out" without access to full student demographic profiles or admin logs, since unrestricted access exposes PII the substitute doesn't need.

Q6. Explain the difference between LDAP-based automated role provisioning and manually assigning access levels, and give one operational benefit of the automated approach.

Show key points
LDAP/SSO provisioning maps directory attributes (e.g., OU=Teachers) directly to Destiny access levels/patron types automatically at login or bulk import; benefit: when a staff member's AD status changes, Destiny updates permissions automatically at next login, eliminating manual role adjustments and reducing the risk of stale/incorrect access.
